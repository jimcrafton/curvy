Hints at the pen

The cursor is where attention is, on a pen display (hand over the screen) and on a separate tablet (hand off-screen). Switch hardware and handedness to see where the chip and the ring need to sit. The bottom bar stays as an expert reference.

  • Variations: A a text chip; B keycaps only (the key is the hint); C a ghost of what the gesture would make, with its measurement; D a small arc of keys round the cursor that becomes a dial when scrubbing; E a coaching card on the paper, away from the pen.
  • Pen resting: after about a second over paper, a chip appears and fades when the pen moves. It retires with the same use counters as the bar.
  • Holding E: the chip names the gesture while the key is down, and the bar repeats it.
  • Scrubbing: the value follows the pen, so the gesture teaches itself.
  • Pen display: the dashed shape is roughly where the hand covers the screen. The chip goes on the other side of the tip, and ring slots under the hand are dimmed. They should hold the least-used commands, or the ring mirrors with handedness.
  • Separate tablet: nothing covers the screen, so the chip sits below and right of the cursor as usual.
