Colour, gradient and stroke editing

Same premise as the rest of the UI: pen in one hand, keyboard in the other, no panels to travel to. Each editor opens at the pen from a held key (or from the object ring), is fully usable with the pen alone, and commits on release as one undo step. The fourth tab is the "..." hub of the object ring: the full appearance list, for when you want everything at once. The widgets in the first three tabs are live; the stroke width profile is a picture only.

Colour

Colour

  • Hold C opens the popup at the pen: hue ring with a saturation/brightness square inside. Drag the ring for hue, drag the square for S and B, release to keep.
  • The preview splits old and new, so a change can be judged against the original without undo.
  • Recent colours sit under the picker and on the number row (1 to 8), the keyboard hand's shortcut.
  • Space toggles fill or stroke while it is open, so one gesture covers both. Hold I to sample a colour from the canvas.
  • The quick H / S / L scrub keys from "Property scrubbing" stay: the popup is for choosing a colour, scrub is for nudging it.

Gradient

  • The gradient is edited on the shape, with its line and stops drawn over it, not in a strip far away. A small card holds the type and the selected stop.
  • Stops are drag targets large enough for a pen. Tap the line to add one, drag one off the line to delete it; no separate add or remove buttons.
  • Selecting a stop shows its hue and lightness sliders in the card; a full colour popup opens with C while a stop is selected.
  • A (hold) scrubs the angle, as in the scrub decision. Linear, radial, angular and freeform share the same stop model; freeform places stops as points instead of a line.

Stroke

  • Hold W to scrub the width (existing behaviour); the card appears fixed at the pen and shows the rest of the stroke on one surface.
  • Cap, corner, dash and alignment are chips: one tap each, no dropdowns. Preview is the real stroke on the canvas.
  • Width profile: Even, Taper, Swell, or Pressure (record the pen's pressure as the profile while drawing). Dragging points on the profile graph for a custom shape comes later; the card shows what the choice does.
  • Dashes get a length/gap pair to scrub once Dash is on (not drawn here).

Docked toolbar and property boxes (the toggles at the top right)

  • Default is the plain canvas. Two switches, both off, bring back a conventional layout: a toolbar on the left with tools and their key letters, and property boxes on the right (Fill, Stroke, Opacity) in the same style as the popups.
  • They are translucent and float over the artwork, so the canvas keeps its full size. They show the same values as the popups, so either route works and they never disagree.
  • The same switches would sit in the settings ring and the Classic workspace path, so mouse users get a familiar layout without changing the pen workflow.

Appearance (the hub)

  • Reached from the "..." under the object ring's name. It is the one place with everything: all fills and strokes as rows, opacity, and later effects.
  • Each row's swatch opens the same colour popup, so there is only one colour editor. Docked to the right edge, hidden with Tab like other panels.
  • Most editing never needs it: C, G, W and the scrub keys cover the common cases from the keyboard.
