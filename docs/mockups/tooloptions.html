Tool options: double-click a tool, hold a slot

Every tool can have defaults for the next shape it makes (a star's points, a rectangle's corner radius, how much a freehand stroke is smoothed). Turn on the toolbar and double-click a tool, or leave it off and tap Space, then hold a ring slot (about 0.4 s), press the barrel button (right-click here) on it, or hold Shift and press its key, or simply tap the option box. A toolbar button with an amber dot has options; a ring slot with a small option box (a tiny dialog with a heavier top edge, as in Maya) has options, and the box is itself a tap target. Line and Zoom have none, so nothing opens. Then pick the tool and drag on the page to see the options at work.

How it works (proposal)

  • Two routes, one card. With the toolbar on, a single click picks a tool and a double-click opens its options next to the button (the Photoshop/Illustrator habit). With the toolbar off there is no button, so the ring takes the same job: a plain tap or flick still picks the tool, while holding the slot (about 0.4 s, a progress bar fills along the slot), the barrel button on it, Shift + its key, or a tap on its option box opens the same card beside the slot. The hub was considered and rejected: with a pen you cannot hover a slot and tap the hub at once.
  • Which tools have options. Marked by a small amber dot on the toolbar button (a bigger glyph there was too much), and on the ring by the option box (borrowed from Maya: a small dialog glyph with a heavier top edge) at the outer end of the slot. On the ring the box is also the quickest route: tap it and the card opens, no hold needed. It sits at the outer end of the slot, away from where a flick lands, so flicking to pick a tool never opens it. No dot or box, nothing happens (Line and Zoom here): no empty card, no error.
  • What they mean. The values are defaults for the next shape, per tool, kept in the settings with the document or the profile. They never change a shape already on the page; that is the HUD's job (it shows the selected node's own parameters). A live shape made after the change simply starts with those values, and the preview in the card redraws as you move a slider.
  • Wired here: Rectangle (corner radius, from centre), Ellipse (from centre), Polygon (sides), Star (points, inner radius), Freehand (smoothing: the fitted curve keeps fewer points as it rises, the badge shows the count). Not wired (the card exists but changes nothing, marked in the card): Select, Pen, Text, Eyedropper.
  • Dismissal. Esc, a click anywhere else, the ×, or picking another tool. The card is the same translucent style as the other floating cards and can sit over the artwork because it is momentary.
  • Open points. Keys: the toolbar prints Pen on P while the ring's P is Polygon; ring keys are only live while the ring is open, so there is no clash, but the toolbar needs its own letter for Polygon (Y here). A hold of a ring slot could instead be the hold-key draw (“hold the letter and drag”); that one is on the keyboard, so the two do not collide. Touch screens would use long-press for both routes.
