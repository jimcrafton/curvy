Rigging: bones, binding, weight painting, IK and a smart-bone dial

A character built from shapes. Pose tool (1): click a bone, drag its tip to rotate it (FK), drag a wrist or ankle ring to pull the limb with IK, drag the hips, drag the Head turn dial. Bone tool (2): drag from a bone’s tip to grow a child bone, or switch to Edit tip to fit the skeleton to the art. Weights tool (3): paint how the left sleeve follows the shoulder, elbow and wrist; pen pressure is the brush strength and the dots show the active bone’s weight. The right arm and the legs are rigid cut-out parts, the left sleeve is smooth-skinned: flip it to Rigid in the Skin tab to see the difference. Hold R and move up/down to rotate the selected bone, K keys it, Enter plays, Space opens the ring.

How it works (proposal)

  • Skeleton = transform nodes. A bone is a transform node with a length, a pivot (its head) and a parent; it attaches at a fraction along the parent bone (shoulders sit near the spine’s tip) with an optional sideways offset. The hierarchy is ordinary graph parenting, so every bone’s rotation is just another channel: it appears in the timeline and dopesheet, keys, copies and ripples like any parameter. The Bone tab shows the path from the root and the children you can step to.
  • Cut-out rig (rigid bind). A part bound to one bone with weight 1 is Toon Boom’s peg: it moves rigidly with its pivot. The right arm and the legs are built this way; cracks at the elbow are the price.
  • Smooth bind. A Bind node takes points, the bone transforms and a per-point weight for each bone, and gives deformed points (linear blend skinning). The weights are a point attribute, the same system as the Bend and Wave weights in the animation bench. The left sleeve is bound to spine, upper arm, forearm and hand; “Auto weights” makes a first pass from distance along the chain and you paint the rest.
  • Weight painting with the pen. Pen pressure drives brush strength (a mouse reads as half pressure), hold B and move up/down to resize the brush, and the dots on the sleeve are a heat map of the active bone (blue none, red full). Add raises the active bone and the others give way so weights keep summing to 1, Subtract does the reverse, Smooth averages with the neighbours. Strokes are one undo step.
  • IK is a solver node. Each limb has an IK amount (keyable, so you can blend FK to IK), a target and a bend direction. Drag a wrist or ankle ring and the limb switches to IK from where it is (no pop); “To FK” bakes the solved angles back into the bone rotations. The solver is analytic two-bone; a chain solver (FABRIK) would serve tails and spines.
  • Smart-bone dial. The Head turn dial is a driver: one control mapped to several things at once. Here it adds rotation to the head and neck and picks one of five face drawings (profile, three-quarter, front, three-quarter, profile) with a stepped switch, so the dial moves bones and swaps drawings together. Each mapping can be turned off or scaled in the Driver tab. This is Moho’s smart bone as a graph node; the pose chips from the animation bench are the same idea stored as a snapshot.
  • Editing the skeleton. In the Bone tool the art is shown unposed so you can fit bones to it: drag from a tip to add a child, or use Edit tip to move a bone (children keep their orientation). Every edit re-binds, so the art stays where it was drawn and only the bones move. A new bone can be given a part with “Bind rigid to bone” in the Skin tab.

What this means for the graph and the code

  • Nodes: Bone (transform with length), Skeleton (collects bones and publishes their world transforms as data), Bind (skinning wrangle, JIT), IK (solver writing angles), Driver (control to many targets through ranges or curves, including int/enum for switches). Bone world transforms are the only new data type; weights ride on point attributes.
  • jitnodes is polyline-only: skinning a bezier path deforms the anchors and handles, which needs the planned bezierPath node. A sleeve like this one stays smooth because it is densely sampled.
  • A rig is a group with published controls (the dial, the IK rings, a master move): the reusable character. Posing a rig is keying those controls, so the dopesheet, poses, copy/paste and the EXR output from the animation bench work on it unchanged.
  • Not here: mesh warp (triangulate, pin, solve), constraints (aim, parent-to-world), bone dynamics, mirroring poses, bone-driven drawing swaps beyond the face, and Smart Bones that read several dials.
