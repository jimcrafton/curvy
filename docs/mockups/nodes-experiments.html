Node graph experiments

Four ways to show the same graph (Star, Transform, Copy, Output) in the minimal UI. All four share one set of values, so scrub a parameter in any of them and the artwork updates; nodes drag. They differ in where the graph lives and how much of the canvas it costs. The bottom-drawer version is in nodes.html.

Graph - Stardrag the title, resize the corner
A - the graph in a movable, resizable translucent window over the artwork - N shows/hides it - it remembers its place
release to hide the graph
B - hold N: the graph appears over the whole canvas on a dimmed artwork; edit nodes with the pen while the key is down, release to go back to drawing
C - each node is a small card on the canvas with its sliders inline, wires between them; no separate graph view, scrub right on the card
D - the shape's own pipeline sits around it; "zoom in" flies into it so the nodes grow and the artwork becomes the display node's thumbnail

What to look for

  • A Floating window - familiar and flexible, and it keeps the whole canvas. Cost: another thing to place and a pen has to move/resize it; best on a big display.
  • B Hold-key lens - fits the hold-key habit already used for scrubbing: the graph exists only while you ask for it and takes the whole canvas, so nodes can be large and pen-friendly. Cost: not for long edits, and you cannot see the unobscured artwork while editing (the dimming is a compromise).
  • C Cards on the canvas - nothing to open, since each node's sliders are right there; suits small graphs (the common live-shape case of 2 to 5 nodes). Cost: large graphs clutter the drawing, so it should fall back to A or B past a handful of nodes.
  • D Zoom into the shape - treats the graph as the inside of the object: select a shape, zoom in, and you are in its node graph (like entering a subnet, which jitnodes already supports); zoom out to return. Cost: a transition to learn; benefit is the same gesture works for groups and nested graphs.

A combination is plausible: C for small graphs and the Peek chip, A or the drawer for medium ones, B as the quick look, D as the way into subnets.
