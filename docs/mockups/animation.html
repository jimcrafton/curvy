Animation: keys, deformers, switches, drawn frames, layers and EXR output

Four objects, three ways to animate. A ball (keyed squash, with a Face child whose mouth and eyes are Switch nodes), a stem (bend and wave deformers weighted along the path) and a bird drawn frame by frame (a Drawings node cycling six drawings, timed on 1s to 4s). Click objects, shift-click to add. Hold S, B, W and move up/down to scrub squash, bend, wave. Tap T for the timeline (a real dopesheet: box-select keys, drag, hold E to scale and G to move them, Ctrl+C/V/D, Ins/Rem frames, a Curves view, and an editable exposure strip for the bird), hold T and move sideways to scrub time, Enter plays, Space opens the ring. Eye, solo and lock live on the timeline rows. Tab opens the full Dopesheet view, horizontal or as a vertical Xsheet spreadsheet. The panel has Node and Output (real multi-layer .exr) tabs. Try + 12 stems in the bar above to see how the timeline copes with many objects.

Two ways to animate in 2D, one graph

  • Rig and pose (cut-out): parameters keyed over time, with deformers. The Ball (squash, position) and the Stem (bend, wave) work like this. Easing is per key (bezier presets, Hold).
  • Switch (drawing substitution, Moho's switch layers): the Face is a child of the Ball, so it follows the ball's position and squash. Its Mouth and Eyes are Switch nodes, an integer picking one of several drawings. The keys are stepped (square keys on the timeline, no easing): a mouth does not tween between Smile and Open. Posing a character is choosing drawings as well as moving bones, so the pose chips can set switches too ("Squashed" makes the mouth Sad).
  • Drawn frames (the xsheet): the Bird is a Drawings node holding six hand-drawn poses. Its timing is a choice, not a curve: 1s, 2s, 3s, 4s hold each drawing for that many frames, as in cel animation. The exposure strip under the Bird's rows is the xsheet laid on its side: one cell per frame with the drawing number, merged where a drawing is held. Turn on onion skin with step 1 and the bird ghosts show the neighbouring drawings.

The dopesheet

  • Selecting keys: click, shift-click, or box-select (drag on empty space; a box over an object or group summary row selects everything under it). All selects every key in view, @Frame every key at the playhead. Clicking a row label selects the row; clicking a summary tick selects all keys at that frame under the object or group. The selection is a set of (parameter, key) pairs, so every operation below works the same on floats, switches and stepped keys.
  • Retime: drag selected keys sideways (they keep their spacing; collisions resolve when you let go). Snap pulls the selection onto the playhead, the range ends and other keys when within a frame; hold Alt to bypass. Hold G and move to shift keys by frames without touching the timeline, hold E and move to scale them about the playhead (the badge shows the percentage); ½ and 2× do it in one click. These holds follow the same gesture family as the property and time scrubs.
  • Copy, paste, duplicate: Ctrl+C copies the selection with its spacing, Ctrl+V pastes at the playhead onto the selected objects of the same type (so a stem’s sway can be pasted onto another stem), Ctrl+D pastes right after the selection (build a cycle). Delete removes keys.
  • Ripple: Ins pushes everything at or after the playhead later by N frames (it refuses when the last key would fall off the end); Rem removes N frames and pulls later keys back. They act on the selected objects, or on everything when nothing is selected, and also shift an editable exposure.
  • Interpolation for a selection: Smooth, Linear or Step in one click (Step makes square, held keys). The key card also has the ease presets and the bezier editor for a single key.
  • Range: [ and ] set the work range (brackets in the ruler, outside shaded). Play loops inside it, the first/last buttons jump to its ends, and the Output tab reports it.
  • Curves: the same selection and tools, but the rows become curves for the selected objects, each normalized to its own range. Drag a key in both axes (frame and value) and it moves the whole selection.
  • The xsheet (exposure strip): bake the bird’s cycle to cells, then click or drag across cells and type 1–6 to set the drawing (the Exposure card has the same buttons), drag a run’s right edge to extend a hold, Hold previous to repeat the drawing before the selection, Back to cycle to discard the manual exposure. This is how drawn animation is timed in Toon Boom and OpenToonz, laid on its side.

Scale: many objects

  • Canvas: draws everything visible. Handles, trajectories, weight dots and onion skin belong to the selection only, so they cost nothing for a big scene.
  • Timeline rows default to Selected: only the keyed parameters of what you have selected, so a 200-object scene still shows a handful of rows. All shows a tree: groups and objects collapse into summary rows that still show when anything keyed under them happens, expand with the arrow, and scroll. Only keyed parameters get rows. Try + 12 stems, then switch to All.
  • Layers tab is an outliner: z-order (top is front), a colour dot per type, eye, solo, lock per object and per group, parent shown for children, shift-click for multi-select. Visibility and solo affect the EXR too.
  • Many of the same thing is not many objects: an array or instance node with per-instance attributes keeps the graph small. The 12 stems here are real objects to stress the UI; a Stems scatter node is the answer for hundreds.

Layers, After Effects style?

  • Yes, as a view over the graph (consistent with "the graph is the document"). A layer is an object: the end of a node chain. Like an AE layer it has order, eye/solo/lock, a parent, and an in/out bar on the timeline: drag the bar's ends to trim when the object exists, drag the middle to slide it (shown in All mode). Unlike AE, the "effects stack" is the node chain you see in the Node tab, and a layer's contents can be any chain.
  • A group is the precomp: a collapsible folder here, a sub-graph with its own time offset and remap in the real app. Blend modes and mattes would be flags that insert Composite nodes.

Output

  • The Output tab is the card of an Output node set to an EXR sequence. Beauty is always RGBA; AOV layers are added: motion (px/frame, per object), id (index of the object in the stack) and tailU, a point attribute of the stems (u or pressure) rasterized as data. View a layer on the canvas with the chips. Download writes a real half-float, uncompressed, multi-layer EXR for the current frame (open it in ExrEdit).

What this means for the graph and the code

  • Key types: float (eased), int/enum (stepped) and later colour and bool. The Switch and Drawings nodes need the int/enum key. jitnodes needs bezier easing and typed keys. The Timing choice is a time-quantize node in front of the Drawings node.
  • Drawings are a small library of shapes owned by the node; the pen tools edit the drawing under the playhead (not in this mock). Palette styles (OpenToonz) are the planned colour nodes.
  • EXR needs more than 8 bits. Blend2D renders 8-bit RGBA, fine for beauty, wrong for data layers. This page writes half-float from 8-bit canvas reads, so the attribute layer has 256 steps and the motion vector is flat per object. Real code needs a float path for AOVs. Beauty is written linear and premultiplied.
  • A per-pixel motion vector falls out of cooking the frame at t and t+1 and interpolating per-vertex differences. Rendering the range cooks each frame (setTime dirties only time-dependent nodes) and writes files on a worker.

Not in this mockup

  • Editing a drawing, tangent handles on the curves (easing is per key), a vertical xsheet view, markers, ripple past the end of the range, lattice and path-deform nodes, morphing between different shapes, group in/out bars, sub-graph time remap, range output (current frame only), EXR compression, per-pixel motion vectors.
