Selection and transform

Click to select (a group selects as one; double-click enters it). Drag empty canvas for a marquee: left to right takes what it fully contains, right to left everything it touches. Drag the body to move (Shift locks an axis, Alt drags a copy); drag a corner or edge handle to scale (Shift keeps proportions, Alt scales from the centre); drag just outside a corner to rotate (Shift = 15° steps, Alt rotates a copy, then Ctrl+D repeats it); Ctrl-drag an edge handle to skew; Ctrl-drag the crosshair to move the pivot (once it is off-centre a plain drag grabs it). Hold R, S or K and move the pen up and down to rotate, scale or skew without touching a handle. Space taps the ring.

How it works (proposal)

  • A transform is a node, not a rewrite. Every object carries one Transform node (translate, rotate, scale, skew as a single affine matrix; the HUD shows it decomposed). Moving, scaling or rotating only edits that matrix, so the shape keeps its clean parameters (a Rectangle stays a Rectangle with width and height) and the move is cheap. Bake pushes the matrix into the geometry and makes a plain path (here a polygon; the real editor would write bezierPath anchors and handles).
  • Groups are subnets with their own Transform. Dragging a group edits only the group's matrix; its children are untouched, and entering the group (double-click, Esc to leave) edits them in the group's space. Everything outside the group dims. Ungroup multiplies the group's matrix into each child.
  • The frame. One object gets an oriented box that follows its own rotation and skew, so scaling stays along its axes. A multi-selection gets the axis-aligned box of everything. Every drag is computed as a delta matrix in world space (frame · scale · frame-1, about the anchor or pivot) and multiplied onto each selected object's start matrix, so a drag never accumulates error and Esc cancels it.
  • Pivot. The crosshair is the pivot for rotate, scale scrubbing and the HUD fields. Ctrl-drag it from the centre (a plain drag works once it is off-centre; it snaps to the nine reference points), or choose a reference point in the HUD; double-click resets it to the centre. The X/Y fields read and set that reference point.
  • Transform again. The last delta is remembered. Ctrl+D duplicates and applies it once more, so Alt-rotate a copy and tap Ctrl+D to build a ring; move-copy and tap Ctrl+D for a row. It starts as a small offset.
  • Smart guides. While moving (and scaling an unrotated frame) edges and centres snap to other objects, the artboard and the optional grid; amber lines show the match. Not mocked: equal-spacing guides, distance labels, snapping to anchors.
  • Scrub keys. Hold R (rotate), S (scale) or K (skew) and move up and down: the fixed card works like the other scrub cards, Shift snaps to 15° / 10% / 5°, release commits as one undo step. They act around the pivot. Letters are placeholders, as elsewhere.
  • Marquee direction. Left to right: contained only. Right to left: touching. No modifier, which suits a pen. (The mock tests bounding boxes; the editor would test the real outline.)
  • Space ring. Over a selection: Group / Ungroup, Duplicate, Delete, Flip H, Flip V, Align… and Arrange… (sub-rings), and a last slot that is Enter group when one group is selected, Exit group when you are inside a group, otherwise Bake. Over empty canvas: Select all, Invert, Deselect, Grid, Smart guides, Scale strokes, Exit group, Reset. The hub pins the HUD, as elsewhere.
  • Strokes. "Scale strokes" off keeps stroke widths constant while the geometry scales (SVG vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke here; in the editor a flag on the Stroke node that divides by the matrix scale).
  • Not mocked: pan and zoom (Space-drag), shear of a multi-selection from the HUD, per-object pivot memory, rotating the multi-selection frame, snapping to anchors, transforming individual anchors (that is Direct select in the pen bench), live-shape handles (corner radius), constraints and numeric expressions in the fields.
